### 2. The Landing Page: The Global Perspective
"When you first launch the application, you are met with our **3D Interactive Globe**. 

**How I did it:**
- I used the `plotly` library in R, specifically the `plot_geo()` function with an **Orthographic projection**.
- Unlike a flat map, this projection provides a realistic sense of scale. I mapped our dataset’s latitude and longitude directly onto this 3D sphere.
- **Dynamic Rotation:** To make the app feel 'alive', I injected a custom **JavaScript snippet** into the R Shiny UI. This script talks to the Plotly engine to auto-rotate the globe by 0.5 degrees every 100 milliseconds, pausing only when a user hovers over a city to inspect it.

**The Plots & The 'Why':**
- **AQI Markers:** Each marker on the globe isn't just a dot. Its **size** and **color** are dynamic. Large, red markers indicate high AQI (pollution), while smaller green markers show cleaner regions. 
- **What it tells us:** At a single glance, a user can identify global 'Hotzones'. You can see the particulate matter density of New Delhi compared to the clear skies of Zurich without clicking a single button."

### 4. The City Dashboard: Deep-Dive Analytics
"The City Dashboard is designed for urban planners and environmental researchers. It’s split into two primary segments: a **Sticky Geospatial Map** and a **Micro-Analytics Explorer**."

#### A. Interactive Leaflet Mapping
"On the left, we have a high-resolution **Leaflet Map**.
- **How:** I used `addCircleMarkers` to plot individual city zones. I integrated a **Dark Matter tile provider** from CartoDB to ensure the vibrant AQI and Traffic markers would 'pop' against the background.
- **The 'Why':** This map provides the context for the charts on the right. If you see a spike in a chart, you can look at the map to see exactly which zone is the culprit."

#### B. AI Risk Intelligence (K-Means Clustering)
"The most technical part of this dashboard is the **AI Risk Intelligence Tab**.
- **How I did it:** I implemented an unsupervised **K-Means Clustering algorithm** directly in the R server using the `stats` package. Before clustering, I used the `scale()` function to normalize variables like PM2.5 and Congestion so they are mathematically comparable.
- **The Visualization:** I used a Plotly scatter plot where the X-axis is PM2.5 and the Y-axis is Congestion. The colors are mapped to the cluster IDs generated by the AI (Low, Moderate, and Hazardous Risk).
- **What it tells us:** This isn't just plotting history; it’s identifying **Anomalies**. It tells us which zones have an 'unnatural' overlap of high traffic and high toxins, helping cities prioritize where to implement green zones or traffic diversions."

#### C. The 40-Chart Extended Telemetry Grid
"Finally, we have what I call the **Extended Telemetry Grid**. 
- **How:** Instead of hard-coding dozens of plots, I wrote a recursive R function in `server/visualizations.R`. This function uses a `switch()` statement to loop through 40 permutations of our data.
- **The Visualizations Used:**
    - **Violin Plots:** To show the distribution and 'density' of noise pollution.
    - **2D Histograms (Heatmaps):** To map the correlation between Humidity and Temperature.
    - **Bubble Charts:** To show three variables at once (X=Speed, Y=NO2, Size=Ozone).
    - **Area Trends:** To show how these variables fluctuate over a simulated time window.
- **What it tells us:** This level of granularity allows us to see, for example, if a specific zone’s noise pollution is a product of traffic speed or just high density. It provides the 'micro-proof' for macro-level environmental trends."
